How the rhythm and repeats work

Geometry first. Each pattern is a periodic network of complete lines and curves. The interlaced design is a geometric reconstruction of your reference, not a crop of the old image. Its rectangular repeat contains two staggered rows.

Stitches second. The engine finds shared junctions, reserves clear space, and fits a whole number of stitches along each interval. Interior stitch-to-gap ratios are preserved; stitch length can change slightly from interval to interval. Junction gaps are intentional exceptions. The actual range above is the one to use.

Edges are not stopping points. Full stitched curves are translated across the boundaries before the output is clipped. Stitch spacing is never restarted merely because it reaches an edge. Wave rows fit a whole number of stitch-and-gap periods to one wavelength.

The tile's box is part of the repeat. Repeat the exported SVG at its exact document width and height, with no spacing, mirroring, or "crop to artwork." Some curves and stitches should be cut by the edge: the next tile completes them. Cropping the SVG tightly to its visible black marks would break the repeat.

Front-side design, not an automatic sewing route. This app does not choose stitch order, optimize back-side carries, or estimate skein length. A gap at a junction does not mean you need to cut the thread. Plan how to traverse the continuous geometry before stitching.

Print scale. Use 100% / actual size, turn off browser headers and footers, and measure the 50 mm ruler. PDF saving is available through your browser's print dialog. SVGs retain millimeter dimensions, but importing applications can rescale them.

STL stencil export. The stencil tool turns each stitch into a rounded through-slot, then extrudes the remaining plastic as a flat plate. Slot width is separate from SVG line weight. Minimum plastic spacing is checked, not silently repaired. Thin areas require acknowledgement; touching slots, detached pieces, or failed mesh checks block export. There is no floor or Z bevel. View both sides in your slicer and test a small print before making a large template.

About the style. These are adjustable design choices, not universal sashiko requirements. See Atsushi Futatsuya's discussion of sashiko rules.
